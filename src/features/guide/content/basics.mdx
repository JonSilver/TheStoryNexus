# Getting Started

The Story Nexus is a writing app first and an AI app second. Connect a model once in Settings, then everything else happens where you write — on a laptop, or under a thumb on a phone.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two minutes to writing**
>
> 1. **Settings → AI providers & models** → paste an API key, or enter the address of a local model server.
> 2. **Story → Open the library** → type a title → **Create**.
> 3. Type a chapter title (or leave it blank) → **Add** → the editor opens.
> 4. Start typing. The **wand** continues your prose; the **sparkles** insert a scene beat.

## 1. Connect an AI model

→ [Open AI providers & models](/settings/providers)

| Provider          | What to enter                                                                                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **OpenAI**        | Your OpenAI API key                                                                                                      |
| **OpenRouter**    | Your OpenRouter API key — one key, many models                                                                           |
| **Google Gemini** | Your Gemini API key                                                                                                      |
| **Local Models**  | The **Server URL** of any OpenAI-compatible server — Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp and the like — then **Refresh Models** |

One provider is enough. Saving a key or URL fetches that provider's models and, if you have no default model yet, picks one for you. Change it under **Default model** at the top of the same page.

Every model list in the app works the same way: type to search by name or provider, and tap the **☆** beside a model to make it a favourite — favourites are listed first, everywhere you choose a model.

> [!NOTE]
> Keys are stored on the server and never sent back to your browser. All AI work runs on the server, which is why a local model works from your phone too.

## 2. Create a story — or bring one in

**Story → Open the library** (or **Switch story** once you have one) lists your stories, grouped by series. Type a title at the bottom and tap **Create**; you land on its chapter list.

To bring in something you've already written, tap **Import** instead — see **Importing a manuscript** below.

## 3. Add chapters

On **Chapters**, type a title (or leave it blank for "Chapter 1", "Chapter 2"…) and tap **Add** — you go straight into the new chapter. Each chapter row has:

- **▲ / ▼** to reorder,
- the **pencil** for chapter details — title, point of view, outline and summary,
- the **bin** to delete (it asks first and tells you how many words you'd lose).

## 4. The writing surface

**Write** is the editor. The chapter name at the top is a dropdown for hopping between chapters. The toolbar:

| Control                             | What it does                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Undo / Redo**                     | History, including a single undo for any accepted AI text                          |
| **Bold / Italic**                   | Inline formatting                                                                  |
| **Sparkles**                        | Insert a scene beat                                                                |
| **Wand**                            | Continue writing from the caret (hidden while text is selected)                    |
| **Rework / Expand / Shorten / Ask** | Appear when text is selected                                                       |
| **Book**                            | The reference panel: search the bible and jot quick notes without leaving the page |
| **… (More tools)**                  | Headings, underline, lists, divider, insert image, summarise chapter               |

On the right: the **word count** and the **save state** — _Saving…_, _Saved_, _Retrying…_ or _Conflict_.

You can also **right-click** selected text (or **long-press** it on a phone or tablet) for Rework, Expand, Shorten, Ask, Cut, Copy, Paste, Bold and Italic.

> [!TIP]
> You never press save. Typing saves about a second later, and the app also saves when you switch chapters or screens, or put the tab in the background. If the same chapter was changed somewhere else, saving pauses and the editor locks with a banner: **Download my version** first, then **Reload** to see the other copy — nothing is overwritten silently.

## Getting around

Four tabs along the bottom:

- **Write** — the editor for the current chapter.
- **Brainstorm** — chats about the story with an AI that has read it.
- **Story** — this story's home, titled with its name: **Chapters**, **Story Bible**, **Notes**, **Read**, **Switch story**; folded below them, **Details, series & voice** and **Export**; and at the bottom, **Duplicate this story…** (an independent copy with its chapters, bible, notes, chats, images and AI settings, in the same series — handy before a big experiment) and **Delete this story…**
- **Settings** — **AI providers & models**, **AI actions**, **Series**, this **Guide**, **Appearance** (light, dark or system), auto-summarise, and **Backup**.

Every screen has its own web address. The browser's **Back** and **Forward** buttons (and the back gesture on Android) move between screens, refreshing keeps you where you are, and you can bookmark a chapter or open its link on another device.

## Importing a manuscript

**Story → Switch story → Import** takes:

- one or more **.txt, .md, .docx or .epub** files — a whole book, or a file per chapter or part, put in order by filename ("part 2" before "part 10");
- or text you **paste** in.

The app splits it into chapters and shows them for review: untick any you don't want, rename them, then **Create story**. Along the way it cleans up the usual debris — email and usenet headers and signatures, hard-wrapped lines, and Project Gutenberg licence boilerplate.

Once imported, **Story Bible → Extract from whole story** builds a bible from the book in one pass.
