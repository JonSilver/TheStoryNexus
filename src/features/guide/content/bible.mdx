# The Story Bible

The story bible is what the app knows about your world — characters, locations, items, events, and more. Its job is to arrive in the AI's context at the right moment without you fetching it.

**Story → Story Bible** shows every entry available to the current story, grouped by category and sorted by name, with a **search** box over names, aliases and descriptions. Mid-chapter, the **book** button in the editor's toolbar opens the same bible as a side panel, so you can look something up without leaving the page.

## How entries reach the AI

Each entry has a **name** and any number of **aliases** (nicknames, titles, other spellings). Before an AI action runs, the server scans the text involved — the chapter, your scene beat, your selection, the brainstorm conversation — for those names and aliases, and adds whatever matches to the prompt.

- **Word boundaries only.** _Ash_ matches "Ash turned away", not "ashtray".
- **Case and accents are normalised.** "MIRIAM" and "Miriam" are the same person.
- **Possessives count.** "Miriam's coat" matches _Miriam_.
- **Multi-word aliases match as phrases.**
- **Disabled entries are skipped.**

When more entries match than the model can take, they're ranked — story before series before global, then by how often they appear — and the rest are left out, the same way every time.

> [!TIP]
> Aliases are the most valuable field. If your narrator calls someone "the sergeant" for three chapters, add _the sergeant_ as an alias, or those chapters won't match the entry.

## Three levels

| Level            | Reaches                   | Good for                                      |
| ---------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **this story**   | One story                 | People, places and things unique to this book |
| **whole series** | Every story in the series | Recurring cast, shared geography, series lore |
| **all stories**  | Everything you write      | Standing world-building and house rules       |

A story sees all three at once. Series entries need the story to be in a series — set that under **Story → Details, series & voice**.

## Writing entries yourself

**+** (New entry) opens the editor: **name**, **category** (character, location, item, event, note, synopsis, starting scenario or timeline), **level**, **description** and **aliases**. Tap any entry to edit it. Series and global entries are shared with other stories, so editing one changes it everywhere; the bin deletes only this story's own entries.

## Letting the AI write them

The buttons along the top of the bible:

### Extract from whole story

Reads every chapter in turn — showing _Chapter 7 of 50_ and how many entries it has found so far — and gathers everything into **one list**, de-duplicated across the book and against your existing bible. Tick what you want and **Add _n_ to bible**. Finds appear as each chapter is read; **Stop** keeps them, and closing asks before discarding anything not yet added. If two chapters in a row fail, it stops and says why rather than spending money on the rest; chapters that failed are named. This is the way to build a bible for an imported book.

### Extract from chapter / Extract from pasted text

The same for the chapter you're on (its name is shown at the top), or for text you paste — an old draft, character notes, a brainstorm reply.

### Scan for updates

Compares the current chapter with your entries and proposes revisions where the facts have moved on. Each shows the old description struck through, the new one and a reason; **Apply** updates that entry.

### Check continuity

Compares the current chapter against the bible and lists contradictions — eye colour, timelines, who knows what — each with the line from the chapter as evidence.

### Merge duplicates

Extraction can produce two entries for the same thing ("Brandon" and "Colonel Brandon"). **Merge duplicates** proposes likely pairs up front — tap one to pick both — or search and pick the two yourself, seeing both descriptions side by side (**⇄** swaps which is kept). **Merge with AI** combines every distinct fact and all the aliases into one entry for you to review and edit; **Save merged entry** updates the kept entry and deletes the other. Only this story's own entries can be merged — series and global entries belong to other stories too.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Nothing the AI proposes is saved on its own. Every extraction, update and merge waits for you to review and approve it. **Look again** runs it once more and adds anything new to what's already there; a merge's **Try again** keeps each result (_Merge 2 of 2_, **‹ ›**), with your edits.
