# Writing with AI

The AI works where you write. **Nothing it produces touches your chapter until you accept it**, nothing it produces is thrown away when you ask for another go, and there are no prompts to pick or context toggles to set — the app gathers the relevant bible entries, chapter summaries and nearby prose for you.

## Continue writing

Tap the **wand**. The continuation streams in **greyed out, right in your chapter** where the caret was — you judge it in context.

- **Keep** turns it into ordinary paragraphs (one undo reverts it).
- **Again** asks for a different continuation. Earlier ones are kept while the chapter is open — flip between them with **‹ ›**.
- **Refine…** — type a steer ("slower, more dread") and press Enter.
- **Dismiss** removes it.
- **Stop** ends a continuation early and keeps what arrived.

If a continuation fails, it stays put and says why, with **Try again** and **Dismiss** — you're not left re-clicking the wand. Anything that arrived before the failure is kept.

## Rework, Expand, Shorten — and Ask

Select some text. The toolbar (or the right-click / long-press menu) offers:

| Action      | What it does                                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Rework**  | Rephrases, keeping the meaning, style and tone                           |
| **Expand**  | Adds detail, atmosphere and texture                                      |
| **Shorten** | Tightens it without losing anything essential                            |
| **Ask**     | Anything else — type an instruction ("make the threat implicit"), **Go** |

The result streams into a sheet. Then:

- **Replace selection** swaps your selection for the result shown.
- If a result comes with the model's own notes — a preamble, a list of revisions, an apology — or runs much longer than your passage, the sheet says so before you use it. **Remove the model's notes** adds an attempt with just the prose; the full reply stays one tap back. Dialogue is never mistaken for notes. Some models, especially roleplay fine-tunes, do this often: **Use a different model for Rework** (or Expand, Shorten…) gives that action a steadier one.
- The sheet names the model that wrote the result, and whether it's your default model or one chosen for that action. **Change model** — also offered on a failure — picks another and tries again with it straight away, as a new attempt. The choice sticks: that action uses it from then on, for all stories, or just this story if the story already had its own choice. Picking **App default** (for a story's own choice, **Same as all stories**) undoes it; **Settings → AI actions** shows and changes it too.
- **Try again** runs the same request again. **Every result is kept** while the sheet is open: _Attempt 2 of 3_, with **‹ ›** to compare — whichever you're looking at is the one **Replace selection** uses. A result cut short — by a failure or by **Stop** — is kept too, marked incomplete.
- **Refine** re-runs it with a steer, as another attempt.
- **Discard** leaves your chapter untouched.

The editor is held read-only while the sheet is open, so the selection can't drift.

## Scene beats

A scene beat is a note to the AI living inline in your chapter: _what should happen here_. Insert one with:

1. the **sparkles** in the toolbar,
2. **`/`** at the start of an empty line, then **Scene Beat**, or
3. **Alt+S** (**Option+S** on a Mac).

Describe the beat — "She finally admits she recognised him at the funeral; he doesn't take it well." Optionally pick a **POV type** and **POV character** (otherwise the chapter's point of view applies). Then **Generate**.

Every generation becomes a **take**. **New take** asks for another; nothing is ever overwritten, even if a generation fails — a take cut short is kept and marked incomplete. Flip between takes with **‹ ›**, delete one you don't want, or type a steer in **Refine** and regenerate.

- **Accept** drops the take you're viewing into the chapter after the beat and keeps the beat (with all its takes) for later.
- **Accept & tidy** does the same and removes the beat.
- The **chevron** collapses a beat; the **bin** deletes it.

You can accept several takes in turn to cherry-pick from each. Beats are scaffolding: they never appear in exports or the word count. The **eye** shows exactly what will be sent to the AI — messages, model, approximate token count — without generating.

## While you wait

Every AI action shows what's happening: _Waiting for the model server to respond… 12s_, then _The model is working on it…_, then the text. A slow start usually means the server is loading the model or busy; **Stop** is always there. If something goes wrong you get a specific message naming the model and server — the **Advanced** guide explains them.

## Chapter summaries

**… → Summarise chapter** writes a short summary of the chapter you're in; **Save summary** stores it. Summaries are how the AI knows what happened in earlier chapters when you write later ones.

With **Settings → Refresh chapter summaries automatically** on, when you've changed a chapter substantially — roughly a paragraph or more, or 5% of a long chapter — and then paused for a minute and a half, it's summarised once in the background. Every generation costs money, so that's all it does: opening or reading a chapter never triggers one, small edits don't, a failed attempt isn't retried until the chapter changes substantially again, and there are at most six an hour. Turn it off if you'd rather summarise by hand. A summary you write yourself — **Story → Chapters → pencil → Summary** — is marked **Keep this summary** and is never replaced automatically. If a background summary fails, a warning tells you why.

## Your story's voice

**Story → Details, series & voice** has a **Story voice & framing** box, up to 2,000 characters:

> Past tense, third person limited. Dry wit, short sentences. British English spelling.

**Derive from my writing** reads passages from the opening, middle and late chapters and drafts one for you: it appears in the box for you to edit, and nothing is saved until you press **Save** (**Discard** keeps your current voice). It's the quickest start for an imported book.

Every prose action follows it — Continue, Rework, Expand, Shorten, Ask, scene beats and Brainstorm. Mechanical actions (summaries, bible extraction) don't, so style rules can't warp the bookkeeping. The **Language** field alongside it (e.g. _British English_) also steers spelling and grammar.

> [!TIP]
> **Facts belong in the bible; voice belongs here.** "Miriam is a retired diver" is a bible entry.

## Brainstorm

The **Brainstorm** tab is a chat with a critical development editor that has read your story — summaries, the chapter you're on, and the bible entries that matter. It will tell you plainly when an idea works against the story, then help you find a better one. **+** starts a new chat; Enter sends, Shift+Enter is a new line, the square stops a reply.

Each reply can go further:

- **Save as note**, **To bible** (extract entries from it, to review and approve), or **To chapter** (a new chapter with the reply as its outline).
- **Regenerate** the latest reply — earlier versions are kept (_Version 2 of 2_, **‹ ›**).
- If a reply fails, **Try again** resends your message — also when you come back to a chat whose last message was never answered. A reply cut short — a failure, **Stop**, or leaving the chat mid-reply — is kept and marked incomplete. If a reply can't be saved, it stays on this device with **Save again** until it is.

## New names

When a capitalised name keeps turning up in your chapter but isn't in the bible, a strip appears: _New name spotted: Okonkwo_. **Add to bible** opens a focused extraction for it; the **×** dismisses it for this story.
